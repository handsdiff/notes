# Alternative Thesis

**Intelligence is getting cheaper. Context is becoming the bottleneck.**

By our estimate, context is already a $500B cost doubling every 18 months. For every $1 spent on tokens reading context, $16 of human time went into producing it. Businesses pay this context tax through meetings, handoffs, search, documentation, prompting, repeated explanations, and work redone because the person or system performing it did not know enough. Much of this context is valuable work. The tax comes from repeatedly compressing, transmitting, and reconstructing it for every new person, tool, and model.

AI is making this problem larger, not smaller. As intelligence per dollar increases, more work can be accelerated by models, more context becomes worth processing, and the cost of missing the right context rises. A weak model with partial context is merely unhelpful. A superintelligent model with partial context is an extremely capable stranger moving quickly in the wrong direction. Today, people compensate by manually rebuilding the relevant state in prompts. This does not scale to a world of persistent agents.

Once these systems begin accumulating assets, making commitments, and initiating liabilities on our behalf, context becomes a prerequisite for delegation. But full context is still insufficient. An agent must understand how a particular person converts information into judgment: what matters now, which constraints remain implicit, which tradeoffs they would make, and which outcomes actually advance their goals. The missing object is not another generic assistant with a larger memory. It is a model of the human using it.

Ordinary computer use already produces the raw material for that model. People read documents, receive messages, inspect model outputs, browse, search, edit notes, write prompts, and change artifacts. These temporally interleaved read-write streams record how inbound information becomes outbound action. Captured at the right boundaries, ordinary work becomes a renewable source of supervision without a separate labeling process.

Next-action prediction is the forcing function. A model that can predict what someone will write, search, edit, or ask next has learned more than their biography or preferences. It has learned something about the moving edge of their work: how they respond to new information and which latent objective organizes the next action. The usefulness of this prediction scales with the intelligence of the underlying model, because a more capable model can extract more structure from the same personal history.

The practical algorithmic path is prediction, recommendation, and agency. Behavior cloning first learns how a person's context becomes action. Samples from that model then become recommendations inside the workflow. The person's continued work supplies a preferred continuation against the model's proposed alternatives, allowing pairwise preference optimization to improve what the system proposes. Continual adaptation keeps the model synchronized with changing context and goals. The result is not merely a copy of the demonstrator, but a human-model system that can generate possibilities the human recognizes as better than what they would have produced alone.

This changes the economics of intelligence. People should spend zero time repeatedly explaining context or restating goals before intelligence becomes useful. A model should remain in touch with the work, understand what is being pursued, and step in with the right action when doing so creates leverage. The same substrate that accelerates an individual can let every person in an organization participate through a model grounded in their own local knowledge and judgment.

These models should belong to the people and companies that produce their context. Frontier providers will supply extraordinary general intelligence, but their economics require them to see only the portion of context needed for a local request. The complete history of how a person or organization thinks is a separate and compounding asset. Its owner can selectively expose slices of it to whichever provider is best for the current objective without surrendering the model that represents them.

The medium-term future consists of billions of these human models coordinating: each carrying different context, goals, and judgment; each operating with superhuman general capabilities; each able to collaborate at machine speed. This is collective intelligence on a scale that dwarfs today's organizations in the same way today's economy dwarfs that of 1900.

Intelligence without context is generic. Context without a model is inert. The people and companies that turn their work into a continuously learning representation of how they think will compound intelligence. Everyone else will rent increasingly powerful systems that still have to be reminded who they are.
